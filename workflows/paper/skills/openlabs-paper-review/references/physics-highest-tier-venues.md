# Explicit highest-tier physics venues

Checked against official journal pages on 2026-08-29.

There is no recognized one-to-one physics analogue of the four leading mathematics journals.
OpenLabs therefore uses a repository-defined benchmark for original physics research. It contains
exactly these three journals:

1. **Physical Review Letters (PRL)** — APS's flagship letter journal. Its current acceptance
   criteria require a significant advance in physical science that opens a research area or avenue,
   makes an essential step on a critical problem, introduces a method of highly significant impact,
   or has unusual intrinsic interest for PRL's broad audience. PRL also requires very high novelty,
   importance, interest, presentation, and a manuscript that stands on its own.
   Official sources: [About PRL](https://journals.aps.org/prl/about) and
   [Information for Authors](https://journals.aps.org/prl/authors).
2. **Physical Review X (PRX)** — APS's highly selective, broad, original-research journal.
   A submission must make a fundamental discovery, define a landmark, establish key connections,
   propel a field in an important new direction, significantly advance the state of the art,
   create a paradigm shift, or provide a high-impact community tool. Innovation, quality,
   long-term impact, and accessibility to physicists outside the specialty are central.
   Official sources: [About PRX](https://journals.aps.org/prx/about) and
   [Information for Authors](https://journals.aps.org/prx/authors).
3. **Nature Physics** — a professional-editor journal for the highest-quality research across pure
   and applied physics. The benchmark asks whether the result is among the most important advances
   in fundamental physics or technological potential, excites experts rather than receiving merely
   a technically positive response, has significance beyond a narrow specialty, and has a credible
   case for fundamental or long-term importance.
   Official sources: [Journal information](https://www.nature.com/nphys/journal-information),
   [submission guidelines](https://www.nature.com/nphys/submission-guidelines), and the journal's
   editorial explanation [Leave to appeal](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys1704).

## Boundary of the benchmark

- **Nature** and **Science** are general-science journals, not physics journals. They may be
  assessed separately when explicitly targeted, but their interdisciplinary editorial filters do
  not define the OpenLabs physics benchmark.
- **Reviews of Modern Physics** publishes reviews rather than ordinary original-research articles
  and is therefore excluded.
- **JHEP**, **Physical Review D**, **Science China Physics, Mechanics & Astronomy**, and other
  specialist or classification-selected journals may be excellent targets, but they are assessed
  through the specialist/CAS-Zone-1 view rather than this highest-tier venue set.

## Required simulated review

Every physics review must independently simulate all three named venues. For each venue it records:

- a 1--10 venue score;
- a five-level journal decision;
- whether the simulation stops at editorial screening or proceeds as an external-review report;
- the official acceptance route that is met, or `none`;
- scores for the venue-specific editorial criteria; and
- a venue-specific rationale.

The benchmark summary is the most favorable credible fit among the three venues, with score used
as the tie-breaker and the fixed order PRL, PRX, Nature Physics as the final deterministic
tie-breaker. This summary does not erase the three individual decisions. The generic CAS Zone 1
assessment remains separate and continues to control ordinary writing readiness.
